# Calibration: what the corpus protects

*What a conversion's calibration data does and does not protect, measured on
Qwen3.8-27B at 4 bpw between 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-25, and the conversational
calibration mix that came out of it. The instrument side — the conversational qbench
slices, and what `template: render` was actually measuring — is in
[qbench.md](qbench.md) "Conversational trace slices".*

**The short version.** EXL3's quantizer holds error down in the activation directions its
calibration exercises, and nowhere else. The bundled corpus is raw text, so it protects
raw text and the model's own chat, and leaves external text sitting inside a
conversation unprotected — which is exactly where a quantized model's quality varied
from draw to draw, and where a pure self-chat corpus (`sc_trace.py`) lost 5x. A
calibration mix that puts the same corpus into conversational form — pasted documents,
tool results, short tool loops, a little of the model's own voice — improves every
conversational slice by 15-32%, improves turboderp's own independent eval trace by
17-18%, and costs nothing on raw text.

## The result: a conversational calibration mix (2026-09-25)

Built with `ctx_trace.py --docs cal --cal_out` in the `yeasah/exllamav3` fork, passed to
`convert.py --cal_data`. Composition, as 250 rows x 2048 tokens:

| slice | rows | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 65 | rows of the bundled default mix, sampled across all its sources in proportion |
| context | 88 | a corpus document pasted into a user turn under an anchoring request ("Summarize this", "What does this code do?"), or returned as a tool result, then the model's own answer |
| loop | 62 | a scripted 2-3 round tool loop (`read_file`, `fetch_url`, `search`) whose results are corpus documents of the matching kind, then the model's answer |
| self | 23 | the model's own voice on `sc_trace.py`'s prompts, excluding the ones the eval slice uses |
| random | 12 | the default mix's random-token rows |

Documents come from the bundled corpus (c4, wiki, technical, code) plus Wikipedia
(20231101, row group 0) in 13 languages. Answers were generated by turboderp's 6.00bpw
with thinking on at medium effort — 242 conversations, 286k context and 177k response
tokens, about 2.5 hours on two 5060 Tis. The shares are provisional and untuned.

Scored against turboderp's published plain 4bpw, as the ratio of excess KLD (below 1 is
better), with 90% row-bootstrap intervals across three conversion draws:

| slice | mixed calibration | td SC 4bpw | our default-cal draws |
|---|---|---|---|
| document in user turn | 0.73-0.77 | 0.89 | 0.96-0.98 |
| document as tool result | 0.68-0.72 | 1.05 | 0.99-1.09 |
| tool loop | 0.75-0.79 | 1.01 | 0.98-1.17 |
| multilingual documents | 0.77-0.82 | 0.94 | 0.96-1.04 |
| own voice | 0.83-0.85 | **0.64** | 0.96-0.98 |
| agentic (2 real sessions) | 0.85-0.96 | 1.03 | 0.98-1.22 |
| **turboderp's own eval trace** | **0.82-0.83** | 0.75 | 0.99-1.01 |

- **Raw text is unchanged:** excess KLD 0.0077-0.0079 against 0.0078 for turboderp's and
  0.0076-0.0080 for the default-calibration draws.
- **Multilingual improved rather than paying for the new formats.** Predominantly
  non-English rows went from 6% (default) to 9%; non-Latin letters from 2.3% to 4.7%.
  European languages are not better covered than before — a weak signal, but the obvious
  place to look when the shares are tuned.
- **All three draws land within a few percent of one another** on every slice, which the
  default calibration did not manage in the one regime where it varied.
- **turboderp's SC keeps its home ground** — the model's own chat, generated by the same
  6bpw that produced SC's calibration trace — and loses or ties everywhere else.

**The one caveat.** Calibration and eval traces share `ctx_trace.py`'s scaffolding: the
anchor phrasings, the three tool definitions, the loop-task wording. Documents and
own-voice prompts are disjoint, checked. The checks that share nothing with the
scaffolding — own voice (15-17%), the agentic sessions from a different harness (4-15%),
and turboderp's trace (17-18%) — say most of the gain is real; the largest gain, on tool
results (28-32%), probably includes some fit to the scaffolding. Separating the two is
`calibration-mix` in TODO.md.

**What this licenses.** One model, one bitrate, three draws. It does not yet say anything
about 2-3 bpw, where the default calibration's draws were furthest from smooth, or about
other models; gemma-4-12B is the obvious second case (below).

## Each calibration protects the regime it contains

The same comparison across the three calibrations measured, by the kind of input:

| calibration | raw text | the model's own chat | external text inside a conversation | external text as the assistant's reply (render) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| default (raw web text) | all draws equal | all draws equal, match td | draws differ by up to 17% (tool results, loops) | a wide lottery, 0.0098-0.0370 |
| SC (own chat only) | — | best, -25 to -36% | equal to plain, or worse | **5x worse** than plain |
| conversational mix | unchanged | better, -15 to -18% | better, -18 to -32% | narrow, 0.0095-0.0133 |

The reading that fits all of it: the Hessians only see the activations the calibration
produces, and the quantizer only controls error in the directions they cover. Outside
them, the error is whatever the rounding and the sign draw happen to put there — large,
or small, and different every draw. That is why a *narrow* calibration shows up as
*variance* rather than as a uniform penalty, and why the variance appears only in the
regime nobody covered.

This is also the measured cost of a monotone corpus that
[qbench.md](qbench.md) "In-domain calibration does not survive contact with other data"
predicted: SC's gain on its own distribution is real, and so is a 5x loss where it is
silent. New sources should enter as slices of a diverse mix.

## How we got here: the Qwen3.8 "instability" (2026-09-22 to -24)

It started as a gap: our locally converted Qwen3.8-27B 4bpw scored 2.4x worse than
turboderp's published one, with every input and the converter version identical, while
our 3bpw beat his. Everything below was measured, in the order it cost least to rule out:

- **Not the converter version, resume, or inputs.** v1.4.2 and v1.4.9 produce
  byte-identical weights on one host; a fresh conversion reproduces a resumed one byte for
  byte; base weights, config, tokenizer, template and calibration corpus all match.
- **Not a kernel.** The gated-delta-net chunk kernel for Qwen3.8's 48/16 head shape runs
  at 1.2-1.3x the bf16 floor, identical across hosts, like the 32/16 control. The
  quantized GEMM is equally accurate on a 36-SM and a 70-SM card (bit-identical weights,
  bitwise-different but equally accurate outputs).
- **Not the platform.** Driver 615 vs 610, nvJitLink 13.4 vs 13.2, and a CPU forced to
  baseline dispatch all reproduce the same conversion byte for byte.
- **A lottery, over sign vectors and rounding.** Injecting turboderp's sign vectors into
  our conversion reproduced his quality on one host and not on the other; alias-free
  signs, other seeds and other hosts landed anywhere from 0.0098 to 0.0370. His 4bpw was
  a good draw, fourth of twelve.
- **Not a bad layer.** Swapping bands between a good and a bad same-sign checkpoint put
  the damage in layers 32-47, mostly the MLPs, co-adapted rather than localized; the last
  blocks, where it becomes visible, are irrelevant when swapped.
- **Only in one framing.** Every number above was scored with qbench's
  `template: render`, which frames arbitrary web text as the assistant's reply. On raw
  text the draws agree within 5%. On turboderp's own eval trace — the model's own chat —
  every one of our draws matches his, the worst included. On the conversational slices,
  the muted, realistic version survives only where documents arrive as tool output.

So the "instability" was the default calibration's coverage gap, exposed by an instrument
that happened to aim at it. The fix is the section above.

## Converter internals worth knowing

**Sign vectors alias within a module.** `convert_model.py` seeds each module with its
index and `quantize_exl3` reseeds per tensor with that same value, so any two same-length
draws at the same position produce identical sign vectors. On Qwen3.8 that makes
`gate.sv == down.su` in every layer, `z.sv == out_proj.su`, and `out_proj.sv == down.sv`
— producer/consumer pairs that incoherence processing assumes are independent.
`quantize_exl3_batch` copies the reuse deliberately, to match the serial path. Parallel
mode (`-d 0,1`) races the shared generator and perturbs the pattern run to run, which is
how published checkpoints come to have mixed draw indices. **No quality effect was
measurable** — aliased and alias-free draws overlapped completely (n = 5 and 4) — but it
is wrong on its face, and a per-tensor seed from a hash of the key fixes it without
breaking resume. Queued as a report in [upstream.md](upstream.md).

**A checkpoint's `suh` carries the negated sign of the drawn `su`**
(`su * in_channel_scales / (-codebook_scale)` in `regularize()`); `svh` keeps `sv`'s sign.
Anything that reads or injects signs has to know.

**Nothing in a checkpoint records its calibration.** `in_channel_scales` is the block RMS
of the rotated weight, not the Hessian, so `|suh|` does not fingerprint the calibration,
and `quantization_config.json` records only rows and columns.

**The converter's own per-layer metrics cannot see this class of damage.** They compare
against states on calibration rows, and calibration rows never separated good draws from
bad at any depth. Same-depth comparisons against bf16 on eval rows — relative residual
error, and a logit lens through the bf16 head — were identical across draws through
layer ~50 and separated only near the output. **A bf16 continuation does see it:** run
bf16's own remaining layers on the quantized state at layer L and measure the final KLD.
At L = 48 that ranked six checkpoints in exactly their final order (n = 6). The damage is
latent in the state and amplified by the model's own later layers.

**Determinism.** A single-GPU conversion is byte-exact across runs, across two GPUs of
one model, and across a resume onto a different GPU. Two GPU models give different
rounding and therefore a different draw — not a worse one.
